# Scouting and recruitment

## Start with a need, not a name

Recruitment works best when the question is specific: a starting left-back, a low-cost backup goalkeeper, an expiring-contract midfielder or a prospect with a credible route to minutes.

**Advice:** define position, acceptable age range, likely fee, weekly wage ceiling and arrival date before searching. Include registration needs: a cheap player you cannot use is not cheap cover.

## Use the Player database

Numeric criteria have **minimum and maximum** bounds. You can filter age, ability, potential, value, wage, remaining contract months, division tier, height, weight, a selected attribute or personality trait, rating, appearances, minutes, goals and assists.

Single-select categories cover nationality, position, age group, preferred foot, fitness, contract status, availability and club geography/competition. A league narrows its club choices; changing a parent filter clears dependent selections.

Core filters are grouped as Player, Ability & fitness and Contract & money. Advanced groups cover Club & competition, Physical & attributes, Personality & traits and Season performance.

Applied chips show what the search actually uses, even with advanced controls collapsed. Remove a chip to undo one criterion or reset everything. Amount inputs accept shorthand such as `750k` and `2.5m`. Reversed ranges are searched in order, and clamped/reordered input is explained inline.

Searches are paginated and sorted on the server. Changing filters returns to the first page. For expensive profile-based criteria, the search checks at most the first 10,000 players passing the other filters in the requested order; a displayed total such as **N+** is a lower bound, not a claim that the entire world was exhaustively counted.

**Advice:** narrow with ordinary criteria first. Country, league, age, position and money bounds make a personality or attribute search more useful and easier to interpret.

### Useful search recipes

| Goal | Start with |
| --- | --- |
| Affordable immediate cover | Position, availability, wage ceiling, value ceiling, fitness |
| Contract opportunity | Contract months at most six, age/position and wages you can afford |
| Development candidate | Youth age range, position, potential, then personality and playing pathway |
| Proven contributor | Competition level, minutes/appearances and rating before goals alone |
| Registration-safe comparison | Nationality and club situation, then each dossier's eligibility assessment |

These are search strategies, not formulas that guarantee successful signings.

## Shortlist before committing

Add candidates to the shortlist and compare alternatives. A shortlist is a planning tool, not a reservation: the player's club and rivals can act while you wait.

Open the dossier to inspect contract, agent, interest, medical and registration context. A value estimate is not a quoted asking price; current wages are not necessarily the demand for a new contract.

For your own players, shortlist actions can open their contract rather than a purchase offer. This lets you keep retention and recruitment priorities in one review process.

## Give scouts a focused assignment

Use **Scouting** to assign scouts to player reports or recruitment searches, with the available country, position, age and budget criteria. A report finishes as game time advances, not while the screen is merely open.

Scout ability, accuracy, detail, risk and bias shape the information returned. Reports include ranges rather than perfect knowledge, plus strengths, weaknesses, risks, projection, tactical fit, resale outlook, personality and agent difficulty.

**Rule:** uncertainty is part of scouting. A broad potential range should not be read as a promise of the top number.

**Advice:** prioritize expensive or consequential decisions for deeper assessment. Compare multiple candidates with similar role and budget assumptions. A modestly rated player whose attributes, personality, interest and pathway fit your club can be a better project than a higher-ceiling player with unresolved risks.

## Interest is a separate constraint

A club agreeing a fee does not mean the player wants the move. The interest model distinguishes the appeal of the move from overall interest after wages.

Move interest below **50** prevents negotiations. Personal-term agreement requires overall interest of at least **85** and meeting the wage floor. The displayed intermediate labels help explain whether the player is open to talks, interested or still needs convincing.

Wages can improve an otherwise plausible move, but cannot override the refusal gate by themselves. Role, career stage, club level, football opportunity and other contextual factors matter.

**Advice:** do not spend the budget forcing one reluctant target when a suitable, interested alternative exists. If you promise an important role, plan how the squad can support it.

## Keep the recruitment loop short

1. Diagnose the squad gap.
2. Filter the database and build a shortlist.
3. Scout the most plausible candidates.
4. Compare fee, wage, contract, interest, health and registration.
5. Negotiate one coherent package while retaining alternatives.
6. After arrival, check registration and assign an actual development or squad pathway.
