# Transfers and contracts

## Choose the right deal

Open **Make an offer** from a player or the Transfers workspace. Available categories depend on the player's situation.

| Route | Purpose and main caution |
| --- | --- |
| Permanent transfer | Buy a contracted player now; agree club and personal terms |
| Transfer with instalments | Spread payment, not total responsibility |
| Free-agent contract | No selling-club fee, but wages and other costs remain |
| Approach to sign (youth) | A youth-specific approach subject to status, compensation and minor rules |
| Future transfer at 18 | Arrange a deferred arrival rather than bypassing an immediate minor restriction |
| Pre-contract agreement | Agree a future move near expiry under the modeled approach rules |
| Loan | Temporary registration with its own terms; see [loans](loans.md) |

The UI only exposes some routes when their prerequisites are satisfied. If a legal route is unavailable, changing the display category does not make the underlying player eligible.

## Follow the stages

The pipeline progresses through **Enquiry**, **Club talks**, **Agent talks**, **Contract review**, **Medical**, **Registration** and **Completed**. Incoming club offers and deferred deals can also show **Offer received** or **Scheduled**.

At Club talks, work on the fee and club agreement. At agent/personal stages, work on wages, role and contract terms. These are separate decisions: a generous fee does not satisfy the player, and a generous wage does not persuade the seller.

Use **Accept offer** only for the terms actually on the table. Editing terms requires **Submit counteroffer**; the interface disables acceptance of a changed draft. **Reject** ends the current offer rather than preserving it indefinitely.

Talks develop over game time and can involve counters, tension, deadlines, breakdowns, leaks or competing interest. Read the current stage and response instead of submitting the same bid repeatedly.

**Advice:** leave enough days for personal terms, medical and completion. A fee agreed on deadline day is not an instant playable arrival.

## Understand the full price

Fee, cash paid today, weekly wage, signing bonus, agent cost, future instalments and retained rights all affect the package. Check both immediate and future affordability.

### Instalments

Choose the upfront share, deferred instalments and payment interval. You still owe unpaid instalments after the player arrives. Lower upfront cash does not erase future commitments or the acquisition's financial-regulation effect.

### Sell-on and buy-back

A sell-on percentage preserves the seller's claim on a later sale. A buy-back establishes a fixed purchase right for its stated validity. Track these in the Transfers workspace rather than treating an old sale as permanently finished business.

**Advice:** accepting future rights can make an acquisition more affordable, but trades away future upside or control. Evaluate the package over the expected career of the player.

### Seller loan-back

An immediate permanent purchase can leave the player with the seller until 30 June, for one year or for two seasons, where permitted. The seller keeps sporting value and may be more flexible on the fee, while you wait for the player.

Loan-backs cannot combine with an onward affiliate loan. International two-season loan-backs are rejected; international minor loan-backs are not a workaround for cross-border minor restrictions. The loan-back must finish before the buyer's new contract ends.

### Part-exchange

During Club talks, **Include a player (part-exchange)** adds an outgoing player to a permanent purchase package. It is not a magic conversion of market value into cash: the combined agreement still has to satisfy the deal's checks.

**Advice:** consider the outgoing player's importance, replacement cost and willingness rather than using a swap to conceal a larger squad hole.

## Personal terms and retention

Negotiate weekly wage, contract length, squad role and signing bonus. Standard contract choices run from one to five years; under-18 players are limited to at most three years. Permanent contracts can include a release clause.

Review existing players' expiry dates before spending all available wages on arrivals. The player's old wage is not a renewal guarantee, and expired contracts can release a player on a free transfer.

Promises and roles create expectations. A deal that fits the accounting budget but cannot deliver its sporting promises can still damage the project.

### Release clauses

Spanish contracts carry release clauses in the modeled system. A release clause is a contractual route to buying the player, not a guarantee of accepted personal terms or immediate eligibility. Read the clause conditions and the action's assessment before committing the fee.

Relegation wage-cut clauses also exist. Relegation can therefore affect the wage bill through contract terms as well as revenue and budget changes; do not assume every wage stays flat.

## Plan future arrivals properly

### Pre-contract agreements

The player must be in the final **183 days** of his current contract, not currently on loan, and at least 18 when the agreement takes effect.

Domestic pre-contract approaches within the **same** UK association are not modeled for England, Scotland, Wales or Northern Ireland. Wait for expiry where that restriction applies; it is not a blanket rule banning every move between different UK associations.

### Future transfers and affiliate pathways

A future transfer at 18 delays the player's arrival; it is not an immediate signing of an otherwise prohibited international minor. Youth approaches may involve training compensation and approval processes.

Some permanent or future acquisitions can include an onward domestic affiliate loan on arrival. That route must meet the affiliate and registration rules then, not merely when you first plan it. An onward placement is distinct from a seller loan-back.

**Advice:** put the actual arrival date in your squad plan. A scheduled player cannot cover this week's fixture, and a future move can reserve financial room before it adds sporting value.

## Incoming offers, rivals and the wider market

Other clubs make transfer and loan offers for your players based on their needs, budgets and wages. **Settings > Simulation > Transfer/Loan Offer Frequency** controls Low, Medium or High activity, with Medium the default; it does not guarantee a particular offer for a particular player.

Accept, reject or counter incoming offers and check reply-by dates. Set availability deliberately rather than expecting the game to know every player you are willing to lose.

Rivals can enter a transfer battle and trigger best-and-final bidding. The relevant panels expose competition and deadlines; your shortlist is not exclusive ownership of a target.

The AI world also buys, sells and loans players between its own clubs. Needs, cash, wages, financial regulations and director policies affect those moves. This can change both your targets and the buyers interested in your surplus players.

## Completion checklist

Before calling a deal a successful signing, confirm:

1. The agreement completed, or note its scheduled start date.
2. The actual employer and playing club are correct.
3. Association and competition registration permit the intended use.
4. The player has a credible role, training plan and path to minutes.
5. Future instalments, rights and loan terms are recorded in your planning.

The historical stage name **Registration** does not mean work permission must have passed before a player can become your employee. Read [signing versus registration](registration.md).
