# Academy and development

## Potential needs a pathway

Growth depends on the gap between ability and potential, age, minutes, environment and professionalism, with further personality, lifestyle and club influences. Potential itself can change. A high projected ceiling is an opportunity to develop, not a result you already own.

**Advice:** give each important prospect a current team, suitable training, a playing-time route and a review date. "Keep him because his potential is high" is not a complete plan.

## Review the Academy

Choose U17, U19 or all youth. Search, position and priority filters surface age-out cases, injuries, fitness, burnout and unpaired players. A care priority includes fitness below 60 or burnout of at least 60.

Age groups use birth-date/season cutoffs, not just a rounded age label. Review next-season age-out cases before rollover and decide whether the player needs promotion, reserve football, a loan or another contract decision.

Annual academy intake is **5-12 players**, shaped by the academy and its wider environment. That does not mean every intake player is first-team quality.

Generation/fill targets and movement caps are different. Typical starting/top-up targets are 25 seniors and 20 per B-team/U19/U17 group. The relevant internal destination caps are 32 seniors, 30 B-team players and 26 per U19/U17 cohort; other competition and eligibility rules can still constrain use.

## Set training deliberately

**Training** has a senior focus, academy focus and overall intensity. Individual plans can override a player's focus and intensity.

Available focuses are Balanced, Technical, Physical, Tactical, Mental, Role, Position, Youth development and Injury rehab. Intensity runs from 0 to 100; the programme labels above 75 as high load/elevated injury risk and below 30 as recovery emphasis.

**Advice:** do not set maximum intensity permanently across a busy squad. Review fitness, burnout, injuries and minutes together. A player carrying match load or recovering from injury has different needs from an unused prospect.

### Learn a secondary position

Choose Position focus and a target position. This is training, not an immediate reassignment of natural ability. Learning takes **`max(6, 15 - floor(adaptability / 10))` weekly updates**, so 6-15 weeks depending on adaptability. Completion creates a competent secondary position and a notification.

Changing the target resets its training counter. Keep the plan stable long enough to finish, and remember that a secondary position still carries a match-selection ability adjustment compared with a natural position.

### Injury rehabilitation

Injuries switch players to Injury rehab. Supported rehab improves their environment and can accelerate recovery, but injury still sharply reduces development; it does not turn absence into a growth advantage.

Check the Medical tab for injury type, severity, context and expected return. An estimated date is a recovery guide, not a reason to ignore fitness immediately after the injury clears.

## Mentorship

Assign mentors from the Academy's Mentorship workflow. Mentor and mentee must be different own-club players, currently at the same club, and the mentor must be older. Cycles are rejected.

The explicit development-environment multiplier is **1.08** for an under-23 mentee with a non-retired mentor at the same club. Weekly personality changes can also be influenced by the mentor. A mentor assignment to an older player does not imply the same youth growth benefit.

**Advice:** consider professionalism, discipline, adaptability and ambition as well as age. A mentor's influence is not inherently positive just because he is experienced. A loan or move separating the pair removes the same-club condition.

## Wellbeing is actionable

Medical shows fitness, morale, anxiety, burnout and other wellbeing measures, plus current and historical injury records.

**Arrange wellbeing support** for an owned player costs **GBP 5,000 in the game's base currency**, requires sufficient cash and has a seven-day per-player cooldown. It reduces anxiety by 10 and burnout by 6, and raises morale by 5 within their bounds.

This is not a substitute for workload management. **Advice:** address the training/minutes problem as well as paying for support, especially when burnout repeatedly returns.

## Call-ups and development requests

Temporary call-ups can last the next match, **1-120 days** or until a date within that range. They retain the contract/wage and return automatically; temporary call-ups end at rollover. Permanent moves are separate.

The coach's call-up fitness minimum is **70**, not the Squad screen's "fit" threshold of 60. Need, merit, form, injury coverage, destination capacity, age and debut context also matter.

With **Coach call-ups** enabled, the coach can act on matchday. With it disabled, proposals appear for you to accept or decline; proposals expire on the fixture date. Ending a coach call-up creates a two-week veto, while declining a proposal creates a one-week veto.

Up to **three active development requests** tell coaches to seek higher-team minutes for selected prospects. They increase opportunity and relax the normal merit gate, but never force a start. Requests can persist across rollover until the player leaves or reaches the first team.

### Debut budgets

First-team debut opportunities have seasonal budgets, counting played and pending debuts. The base is generally two for elite/mid top-flight clubs and three for lower-standing/top-flight or lower-tier situations. Strong academies, development philosophy and injury crises can increase the permitted budget within the modeled limits.

The match context still matters: protected late-substitute opportunities, safe scorelines, dead rubbers and suitable cup fixtures are different from demanding that a youngster start a decisive match.

**Advice:** use a progression ladder: youth football, appropriate higher cohort/reserve exposure, a development request or temporary call-up, then sustained senior minutes when ready. A legal senior age floor is not proof of readiness.

## Develop the staff as well as the players

**Staff** contains Staff team, Director assistant, Relationships & politics and Recruit staff.

Hiring an unattached candidate costs **12 times weekly wage** upfront, then the ongoing wage. Only one sporting director assistant can be employed; release the current one before replacing him.

A staff development course costs **GBP 50,000** and improves ability, communication and tactical knowledge by one, capped at 99. Releasing staff costs **26 times weekly wage** and cancels that staff member's active scouting assignments. Check those consequences before treating a replacement as cheap.

**Mediate** in Relationships & politics improves staff relationships, morale and club chemistry; it is not an ability-training action.

### Delegate with limits

The sporting director assistant can provide weekly briefings, shortlist targets, assign scouting within the weekly allowance, prepare draft renewals, manage individual development and monitor loans.

Employ the assistant, enable duties, set a weekly budget and inspect the resulting reports/actions. Without an assistant, delegated work is paused. Prepared renewals still need your signature; loan monitoring does not automatically recall players.

**Advice:** delegate routine coverage, not accountability. Check reports before assuming a contract is signed, a player has been scouted or a struggling loanee has returned.
