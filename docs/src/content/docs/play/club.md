# Finances and the living club

## Money has several meanings

| Measure | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| Cash balance | The club's available cash position |
| Transfer budget | The allocation the board lets you commit to acquisitions/investments |
| Weekly wage budget | The permitted player-wage envelope |
| Operating revenue/costs | Whether ordinary football operations are sustainable |
| Financial-regulation assessment | Whether modeled monitored costs/losses fit the applicable rules |
| Squad market value | An estimate of player assets, not guaranteed sale receipts |
| Club enterprise estimate | A revenue-based club valuation, not cash you can spend |

Open **Finances** for Overview, Ledger, FFP and Investments. Overview includes staff wages, annual wage commitment, amortisation, season investment and cash movements. Ledger explains dated receipts and payments.

**Advice:** inspect the ledger when cash moves unexpectedly. A wage split, instalment, bonus, agent fee or infrastructure repayment can matter even when no new transfer completed that day.

## Adjust budgets without inventing money

**Adjust budgets** reallocates transfer and weekly wage room. The revised transfer allocation must remain non-negative and the revised wage budget must cover current wages.

Moving the slider does not create cash or remove operating costs. It also does not cancel wages, future instalments or financial-regulation obligations.

Staff wages are separately visible and affect operating affordability. A support team can improve the sporting environment while still being too expensive for the revenue base.

## Financial regulations

The FFP view distinguishes forecast accounts from closed periods and shows football revenue, squad costs, squad-cost ratio/headroom, rolling earnings, allowable loss, observed monitoring periods, amortisation, sanctions and contingent purchase commitments.

Acquisition costs are amortised over the contract for regulatory accounting, capped at five years. A player sale's profit uses remaining book value, not simply the entire fee. Cash flow and regulatory profit are therefore different.

Where configured, continental monitoring includes UEFA's modeled **70% squad-cost limit**. Domestic regimes are not all identical. Where a national threshold is not verified, the game does not invent a universal national FFP loss allowance: local solvency and club budgets still matter.

Equity-supported allowable loss is an assessment, not a button that lets the director inject unlimited money. There is no general FFP top-up, pay-fine or automatic appeal control.

Sanctions can include settlements, levies/fines, point deductions and registration embargoes. AI clubs also face financial gates; their market activity is not unlimited spending.

### Watch negative cash early

The cash-based registration embargo triggers when balance is **strictly below negative four weeks of the weekly wage budget**, and this particular embargo lifts only when balance is non-negative again. Other embargoes have separate causes.

For example, a GBP 100,000 weekly wage budget gives a cash-embargo trigger below GBP -400,000, not below GBP +400,000. Returning to GBP -100,000 does not yet lift it.

**Advice:** act before the threshold. Renew fewer marginal players, reduce wage commitments, sell deliberately and protect cash for instalments. Waiting for an owner rescue is not a financial plan.

## Invest in the environment

There are **two separate infrastructure systems**.

### Direct sporting investment

In **Finances > Investments**, **Approve investment** improves Academy development or Training facilities by **three rating points**, capped at 99.

The displayed cost includes the finance-skill discount. Both cash balance and transfer budget must cover it, and the transfer allocation is reduced. Do not promise the same money to a signing.

### Board infrastructure projects

Club Life shows larger projects: training facilities, academy work, stadium expansion and new stadiums. The board plans and progresses these projects based on need, demand, ambition and finances. They can use cash or debt, creating weekly repayments.

You do not directly commission a new stadium through the small investment button. Projects have timelines and can be halted/cancelled in administration.

**Advice:** compare a marginal signing with a lasting environment improvement, but preserve enough immediate depth and liquidity to survive the season.

## Manage the board relationship

**Boardroom** shows confidence, pressure, crisis, hype, league objective, manager trust, culture, academy, financial position and your relationship/reputation.

Available approaches include presenting a youth pathway, backing the manager, requesting transfer funds and submitting a recovery plan.

These are not harmless dialogue options:

- A youth pathway creates a **five-week review of youth minutes**; it does not force the coach to select youngsters immediately.
- A recovery plan buys time until a **five-week crisis review**; it is not an immediate reset of the crisis meter.
- A successful transfer-funds request increases pressure and makes the required finishing position one place harder, as well as allocating money.
- Backing the manager affects the relationship context; it does not guarantee job security or improved results.

**Advice:** only make a promise with an executable plan. Pair a youth pledge with suitable training, development requests and realistic opportunities. Pair a recovery pledge with changes that address the actual squad and financial causes.

## Media, fans and unexpected events

**Media & fans** contains supporter discussion and press coverage. Journalists, narratives, sentiment and response choices connect results, transfers and club relationships. Press interaction uses the offered responses, not a free-form conversation simulator.

Unexpected events appear with deadlines, possible responses and stated effects on cash, morale, fitness, fan mood, board pressure, manager trust or individual players. If an event expires, its default choice applies. Unaffordable cash-cost choices are disabled.

Read event history to distinguish your selected response from an expired/default outcome.

**Advice:** do not advance four weeks while a consequential event awaits a decision. A quiet notification setting does not freeze supporter sentiment, contracts or board commitments.

## Ownership and the surrounding world

Open a club's **Ownership** view to inspect its owner, type, wealth, stake/member control, ambition, patience, acquisition debt, group/sister clubs, takeover activity, elections and history.

Owner types include local owners, billionaires, state-backed funds, private equity and members. Takeovers, group acquisitions and member elections happen in the simulation. The director does not directly buy the club or choose its next owner.

Germany's modeled 50+1 rule and member-control rules in Sweden/Norway restrict ownership structures, with modeled exemptions. UEFA multi-club control can create blind-trust or exclusion consequences when controlled clubs qualify for the same competition. Common ownership is not unrestricted access to cheap players or a guaranteed European place; related-party pricing constraints apply.

The **World football** feed and club records show AI clubs' manager/director changes, recruitment policy, affiliates, infrastructure, administration and ownership. A new manager can change formation/style, altering what the club needs from the squad.

**Advice:** recheck a target club after a manager change, takeover or relegation. Yesterday's willing seller or borrower may have a different policy and budget today.

## Administration is not a soft reset

The insolvency model considers balance after transfer instalments due within 91 days. Its tolerance is the greater of 40% of season revenue and GBP 250,000; persistent breaches over ten weekly checks can trigger administration.

Administration can freeze transfer budgets, impose an embargo and point deduction, halt projects, force sale listings and put the club into a sale process. Administrators' sale listings cannot simply be unticked while administration continues.

Deductions vary by modeled association; do not assume every club receives the English penalty. Exiting administration requires the modeled recovery or creditor process, not merely clicking a board response.

Owner rescue can occur under its own wealth, patience and timing conditions, but is not guaranteed. **Advice:** keep a cash forecast that includes instalments and returning loanees so you can make controlled sales before administrators make them for you.

## Your director can grow or lose the job

Settings exposes scouting, negotiations, development, finance and leadership skills. Spend available points with **Develop**. Skills cap at five; disabled skill-tree mode prevents spending. In specialist mode, levels above two have experience gates: raising a skill from two to three requires 200 experience, three to four requires 300 and four to five requires 400.

New-career background gives its starting specialization; later experience can earn further skill points. Develop skills that support the project rather than assuming every setting is cosmetic.

If your tenure ends, Continue stops until you accept another club role. The next appointment is reputation-constrained: a club more than 25 reputation points above your director is not offering the job. You cannot freely change employers during an active tenure through the profile editor.
