# Your first career

## Before you begin

On a hosted installation, sign in with the identity provider configured by the host. A new non-admin player may need approval: the waiting screen is not a broken career. An administrator must review the access request; there is no automatic approval email.

Your hosted account has its own world and save catalog. Your signed-in identity and your fictional sporting director are separate: changing the director's name does not change your account.

In local development, sign-in may be disabled. That mode is not evidence that a public hosted installation allows anonymous play.

## Create the appointment

From the start screen, choose **New career**. A new account's football world is prepared explicitly at this point, not merely by signing in. Preparation can take several minutes; keep the page open while the busy screen reconnects.

Choose a career name, a director name, a professional background and a senior club. Search and filter the club list by country and division. Review the club rather than assuming a famous city means a wealthy or strong team: the world is fictional.

Your initial background gives two starting points in one director skill:

| Background | Starting specialization |
| --- | --- |
| Former scout | Scouting |
| Former player | Leadership |
| Negotiator | Negotiations |
| Finance executive | Finance |
| Academy director | Development |

This is the initial skill allocation, not a permanent ban on other activities. Editing a background later does not repeat the new-career skill initialization.

**Advice:** for a first career, favor a club whose budgets, squad depth and expectations leave room to learn. A smaller club is not automatically easier: it may have less room to absorb a bad contract.

Starting or loading another career preserves the active career in a manual snapshot first. Read any replacement warning, because manual-save retention still applies. See [time and saves](time-and-saves.md).

## Your first hour

### 1. Read the Overview and Inbox

Overview summarizes the transfer budget, wage headroom, squad situation, fixtures, standings and recent news. Open linked player, club, competition and match records rather than treating the summary as the full report.

Inbox is your work queue. Read actionable messages: a loan offer, expiring negotiation, event decision and routine news story require different responses. Notifications can be filtered in Settings, but hiding a topic is not the same as resolving its underlying problem.

### 2. Establish what the manager needs

Open **Squad**. Inspect first-team and reserve groups, then Senior, U19 and U17 cohorts where available. Review position coverage and readiness, not just average ability.

The manager fills the club's formation using primary positions first, secondary positions second and emergency alternatives last. The match-selection conversion uses full ability in the primary position, 94% in the secondary position and 80% in an unfamiliar position. A higher headline ability does not make every player equally suitable everywhere.

Check injuries, fitness, suspensions, international absence and registration before deciding that a position is covered. An unavailable star cannot solve today's shortage.

### 3. Understand the money

Open **Finances** before making an offer. Cash, transfer budget, wage budget and financial-regulation headroom are different constraints. An instalment deal lowers today's cash payment but creates future commitments; a free agent still needs wages and may cost agent fees.

Record a small set of priorities: for example, one immediate first-team gap, one renewal and one development pathway. Avoid committing the entire budget before finding out what those priorities cost.

### 4. Review the future

Open **Academy**, **Training** and **Staff**. Identify prospects who need a plan, older players who could mentor, positions with no successor and contracts approaching expiry. Check whether a sporting director assistant is available before expecting delegation to do anything.

### 5. Build a shortlist before buying

Use **Player database** to find candidates, shortlist them and request scouting. Compare actual fit, report confidence, interest, likely cost and registration risk. Do not equate market value with the seller's asking price.

### 6. Advance a day and review the consequences

Select **1 day** beside Continue for your first few actions. Scouting and negotiations take game time; clicking around the interface does not complete them. After advancing, return to Inbox and the relevant screen.

## Navigating efficiently

The sidebar groups the game into Workspace, Squad & development, Recruitment, World and Club. Player names open dossiers; club names open club sheets; fixtures open Match centre. Competition records include tables, fixtures, scorers, qualification and history.

Use [the squad guide](squad.md) for player dossiers, [scouting](scouting.md) for your search process and [season planning](strategy.md) once the initial audit is complete.
